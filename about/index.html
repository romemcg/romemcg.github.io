About

The story behind the resume.

I started close to the hardware and moved up the stack. The industries changed. The discipline did not. Tap any chapter to open it.

Engineer by training. Builder by curiosity.

I am a Filipino electronics engineer from Batangas, now based in the UAE. I moved from hardware into IoT and smart city platforms, and I now build my own products on the side. The industries changed. The way I work did not: understand the problem, then make it better.

Beyond work

Get to know me.

A small-town kid from Batangas, still figuring out how far life can take him.

I grew up in Batangas, Philippines, with big dreams and no clear idea where life would lead me. Along the way, I became an Eagle Scout, earned my engineering license, completed my MBA, and eventually found myself building a career thousands of kilometers from home, in the UAE.

But those are just the milestones, not the whole story.

I've always been the quiet type. More comfortable observing, listening, and figuring things out than being the loudest person in the room. I'm curious by nature, constantly learning, and sometimes guilty of overthinking before taking the first step.

My journey hasn't been a straight line. I've changed directions, started over, made mistakes, and discovered interests I never imagined I'd pursue. From engineering and technology to building products, exploring business ideas, improving my health, and learning how to enjoy life beyond work.

I'm still learning to speak up, put myself out there, and share the journey instead of waiting until everything is perfect.

This is more than my resume. It's the story of where I started, what I've learned, and the person I'm still becoming.

How I work

Quiet and independent, by default.

When I am focused on a problem I can go deep and keep moving without always sharing enough along the way. I have learned that people need visibility and context. So I share progress earlier and ask for input before things feel perfect.

What I value

Understand the problem first.

Understand the product, the people using it and the problem. Then keep making it better. I try not to hide behind frameworks and vocabulary.
